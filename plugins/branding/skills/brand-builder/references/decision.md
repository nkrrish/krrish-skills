# Helping the user choose

## The comparison table

One row per check, one column per finalist:

| Check | What to show |
|---|---|
| Exact .com | free with price, taken (by whom, live or parked), or a prefix domain they own |
| Trademark | Low, Medium or High, with the closest mark and its classes |
| Live products with the name | none, or what they are and how close to the category |
| Handles | which are free, taken, unknown |
| Hear it, spell it | exact out of 24, top mishearings, whether those domains are free |
| Search today | empty, light, crowded (and with what) |
| Meaning | says what the product does, partly, or nothing |
| Pitch read | how a founder or investor hears it in five seconds |

## How to rank

1. **Ownership first**: exact .com, no live product with the same name nearby, low
   trademark risk, search results you can own. These cannot be fixed with design.
2. **Then credibility**: does it sound like a real company, or like a medicine, a toy, a
   competitor, a typo?
3. **Then memorability and meaning**: can someone spell it after hearing it once? Does it
   help explain the product?
4. Look and sound come last: a design system can be built for any name.

Say what branding can fix (sound, look, meaning, perceived category) and what it cannot (a
live product with the same name, a taken exact domain, crowded search, a trademark on the
root). Give one recommendation and the conditions that would change it (for example "if
noodl.com can be bought and the trademark search is clear, noodl moves to first").

## Investor and acquirer view

- An exact .com and a clean coined mark read as a company that owns its name.
- A prefix domain (itsname.com, getname.com) reads as "could not get the name". Fine early,
  expensive to fix later.
- A live product with the same name is a due-diligence flag and a cease-and-desist risk.
- A descriptive word with a letter added (RecapQ) is a weak mark in a crowded category.

## Before committing

- Ask five people who have not seen the name to hear it once and type it.
- Ask a trademark attorney to clear it in the user's markets.
- Buy the exact .com and the common misspellings as redirects, only with an explicit yes.
